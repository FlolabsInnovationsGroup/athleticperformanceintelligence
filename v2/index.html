Every Athlete Has a Unique Peak Performance Signature

An athlete's physical condition, mental readiness, environment and performance history relate to one another. By looking beyond individual measurements, teams can build a more complete picture of what supports each athlete's performance.

Body

Sleep, recovery, nutrition, hydration, physiology and body composition.

Mind

Cognition, psychology, confidence and competitive arousal.

Context

Travel, environment, routines and relevant life circumstances.

Performance

Training load, sport-specific output and competition results.

Understand What’s Normal for Each Athlete

An athlete's measurements are most meaningful when considered alongside their own history. Reviewing changes in sleep, recovery, cognitive performance, training and other relevant factors can help staff distinguish an unusual change from an athlete's typical patterns.

Recognize Changes Before They Show Up in Results

Athlete Scenario 1

An athlete reports feeling ready, but their recent sleep, recovery measurements and reaction-time results differ from their usual patterns.

What staff can do:Review the measurements together, check data quality, speak with the athlete and decide whether a change in recovery or further evaluation is appropriate.

Athlete Scenario 2

An athlete starts performing below their usual level, even though their training routine hasn't changed much. They're also feeling more tired than usual and their recovery data shows they're not bouncing back as they normally do.

What staff can do:Talk with the athlete about how they're feeling, review their recent training and recovery and decide whether they may need more rest or an adjustment to their training.
